# Liability Threshold Model

## Overview

The modern study of common diseases assumes that the genetic contribution to common disease is quantitative and continuous, so that any given genetic variant typically will have a small effect on disease risk, and only by the additive aggregation of  many genetic variants can disease risk be significantly affected. In contrast, many common disease phenotypes are most naturally modeled as binary variables: a person either has the disease or does not.  How can we bridge the gap between our theory of continuous genetic risk and our observations of binary phenotypes? The liability threshold model is simple, popular approach.


Under the liability threshold model, genetic contributions to disease risk $G$ combine additivity with environmental contributions $E$ to produce the latent disease liability variable $L$. When $L$ exceeds the threshold $Y$, the patient has the disease phenotype $Y=1$.  Otherwise, the patient has the normal phenotype $Y=0$
